01 / The experience

A cautious expedition, one contact at a time.

Playable foundation

Secure the crash site

Two intruders are inside the border. Read their windups, dodge committed lunges, and recover salvage from the partly failed ship.

The mountaintop is your first territory: two buildings, a power station, a cargo breach, and a gated exit.

Prepare at the outpost

Restore power, recover health, and install rifle or armor upgrades. Choose attributes when Command approves a new circle.

Recovery matters. Active threats and a six-second grace period block free healing at the boundary.

Hunt beyond the gate

Scout a creature, commit to a fight, then decide whether you can afford another. Return with salvage, field XP, and practiced skills.

Target a 28–45 second opening encounter, with stronger fights demanding more decisions. This is a playtest target, not a measured live-fight guarantee.

The tension: shooting costs stamina; moving reduces shot damage; sprinting stops fire. A reload is an opening for the creature. A second pull adds another lunge to read, rather than merely inflating the first creature’s health.

02 / Advancement

Experience opens the door. Practice earns the circle.

Field XP is cumulative and never spent on promotion. You also need the required skill ranks and mission milestones, then report to Command.

A concrete first promotion

Two intruders + one field Frostling

24 + 24 + 32 = 80 field XP. Three all-rifle kills supply 54 weapon training XP, enough for Rifles 3; Circle 2 needs rank 2. Four resolved strikes can supply the 12 XP for Defense 1.

Three kills grant 24 Survival XP, enough for Survival 1. The first power repair grants 12 Engineering XP, exactly rank 1.

Weapon credit is shared with melee. Using both weapons splits the 18-XP budget. Evasions count toward Defense; taking damage is optional.

A concrete Circle 3 route

A hunting trip and equipment work

After reaching 80 XP, two Voltskitters, one Murkstrain, and one Frostling add 40 + 40 + 48 + 32 = 160, reaching 240.

Seven total all-rifle kills supply 126 training XP (Rifles 5). Seven kills supply 56 Survival XP (rank 3). Eighteen resolved strikes across those lives can supply 54 Defense XP (rank 3).

Engineering 2 requires 30 total XP: the repair (12), two wreck recoveries (10), and one upgrade (8) meet it exactly. These are possible routes, not kill-count-only promotion guarantees.

    Attributes are a different budget

    Spend the three points from a promotion on persistent character stats. Talent points in the proposed tree below are a separate currency.

    Rifle damage = 8 + Rifles rank × 0.4 + Power + rifle tier × 2. Melee damage = 13 + Melee rank × 0.4 + Power. Combat stamina recovers at 3/s; outside combat it recovers at 10/s.

    Secondary attacks & action bar

    Proposed interaction

    Equip one weapon secondary, one defense action, and one utility action. Keep rifle fire, melee, reload, and field patch as dedicated base controls.

    Proposed controller layer: D-pad Left / Right cycles the three equipped secondary slots; L2 activates the selected slot. L1 keeps cycling targets. Keyboard: 7 / 8 / 9. D-pad navigation remains available while menus are open. Display ammo, stamina cost, cooldown, and unavailable reason in the hotbar.

    L2 is reserved for this proposal; controller mapping requires an in-game conflict and accessibility pass. Use target lock or an explicit aiming state, never a hidden target-selection rule.

    The ability contract

    1. Validate target, range, line of sight, equipment, cooldown, and stamina.
    2. Reserve resources only when the action begins; recheck each scheduled burst shot.
    3. Apply animation, hit timing, VFX, and sound from the same action event.
    4. Resolve damage and interrupt rules once; grant skill credit through existing caps.
    5. Expose remaining cooldown and failure reason to the HUD.

    Today rifle/melee hits are immediate; enemy hits land after their windup. Planned animation-driven player hit events must preserve responsive input and measured fight pacing.

    06 / Contact dossier

    A strain is an identity. A roll is an individual.

    Keep the four recognizable creature silhouettes. Roll bounded stats and occasional readable traits within each strain, rather than turning every spawn into a different species.

    Fixed rewards live

    ContactThreatHPStrikeSpeed m/sXP nowNormal roll XP*Salvage

    *Proposed rolls: HP ±10%, strike ±8%, speed ±5%. Harder rolls add XP; easier rolls retain base XP. The two tutorial intruders stay fixed at 24 XP each. Recommended threat circle is guidance, not an entry lock.

    Interactive proposal · not game code

    Roll a creature

    Baseline manual scenario: fixed stats and a Normal trait. Apply seed or New random spawn creates a reproducible rolled creature. Moving sliders creates a manual scenario.

    Reward the actual challenge

    Use rounded spawned stats to calculate a threat score:

    T = 0.55 × HP ratio + 0.30 × strike ratio + 0.15 × speed ratio

    XP = ceil(base XP × max(1, T) × mechanic bonus)

    Ratios compare the rolled creature to its strain’s fixed baseline. HP gets the largest weight because it extends exposure to attack cycles. Strike and speed increase mistakes and positioning risk.

    Normal · 90% of field spawns

    No special mechanic. Mechanic bonus 1.00. Independent bounded stat rolls yield Frostling 32–35 XP, Voltskitter 40–44, Emberclaw 52–57, and Murkstrain 48–53.

    Plated · 7% of field spawns

    HP ×1.25, strike ×1.10. A visibly armored carapace gains frontal guard: shots arriving within the front 120° deal 90% damage; flank shots remain full damage. Mechanic XP bonus ×1.10 accounts for that positioning tax. Plan 38–45 XP for a Frostling, subject to exact rounded rolls.

    Volatile · 3% of field spawns

    HP ×1.15, strike ×1.15, speed ×1.10. A glowing dorsal sac telegraphs a second committed lunge after a 0.8-second windup; only successful windups can start the follow-up. Mechanic XP bonus ×1.15. No instantaneous untelegraphed damage.

    Generate the roll once per creature life. Save its seed, stats, trait, and training budget; leashing never rerolls it. Respawn creates a new life and roll. Award kill XP exactly once. Keep perimeter encounters fixed so onboarding remains predictable.

    The proportions and weights are initial tuning values. Practice XP remains capped at 18 shared weapon XP and 18 Defense XP per life; affixes do not multiply training credit.

    Encounter cadence estimator

    Stationary, no incoming attacks, misses, or movement. Uses the rolled HP above, constant selected rank, immediate hits, and full magazine. Plated guard is assumed bypassed by flanking.

    Measured current-game baseline

    28.25 seconds

    25 shots · 3 reloads

    Measured in Godot against a stationary 200-HP Frostling with incoming attacks and movement disabled. Rank training remained active; this is a firing/reload benchmark.

    The planner freezes rank for a transparent estimate. Formula: (shots − 1) × 0.9 + reloads × 2.2. Rank-ups during combat, movement, dodges, target access, and frame timing can change the live result.

    Field contacts respawn after 45 seconds; cleared perimeter intruders do not. Leashed creatures recover full health, so repeated partial pulls cannot chip them down or refill their training budgets.

    07 / Equipment & enhancements

    Salvage should change what you carry.

    Stat upgrades implemented

    Current armory economy

    • Each installed upgrade costs 4 salvage and grants 8 Engineering XP.
    • Upgrade order alternates: rifle Mk II → armor Mk II → rifle Mk III → armor Mk III.
    • Each rifle tier adds 2 damage. Each armor tier adds 8 percentage points mitigation.
    • Total reduction: min(40%, Defense rank × 2.5% + armor tier × 8%).
    • First power repair costs 2 salvage. Three wreck recoveries supply 2 salvage each, 30 seconds apart.
    • Defeat extracts you to the outpost and removes 2 salvage; no XP debt.

    Today gear tiers change stats; new pieces do not yet visibly replace the player’s equipment. There is no full inventory or finite ammo reserve.

    Equipment proposal

    Visible slots

    Primary rifle, sidearm, torso armor, helmet, backpack, and utility module. Modules appear on the model, with clear Mk I / II / III silhouettes.

    Start with rifle + torso + utility. Each item has one main stat and at most one perk; avoid unreadable random stat piles.

    Enhancement proposal

    Choose a tradeoff

    A magwell enables Fast magazine. A stabilizer helps aimed fire but adds weight. A shield module enables Field shield at its talent gate.

    Talents authorize abilities; installed modules supply hardware. Show missing hardware separately from missing skill or circle requirements.

    Loot proposal

    Bounded reward rolls

    Keep base salvage guaranteed. Plated or Volatile contacts roll a 25% chance for +1 salvage and a future trait-specific crafting component.

    Persist the loot roll with the creature seed and consume it once on defeat. No equipment drop rates until the item catalogue and upgrade costs are tested.

    08 / Implementation order

    Build the complete loop before widening the world.

    Next development phases

    Creature rolls + reward receipt

    Implement the seed, stat bounds, trait visuals, and once-per-life XP/loot receipt. Keep the two intruders deterministic.

    Acceptance: save/load recreates the same creature; leash cannot reroll; kill credit cannot duplicate; normal XP and tutorial totals match this guide.

    A usable MMO action bar

    Add target information, secondary slots, controller slot selection, cooldowns, costs, and failure reasons. Start with Disruptor shot and Quickstep.

    Acceptance: keyboard and PlayStation can equip, activate, cancel, and inspect abilities without conflicting with base actions.

    Talent points + visible equipment

    Persist talent choices separately from attributes. Install physical modules and gear variants. Animate a detached magazine swap and terrain-aware foot placement.

    Acceptance: no invalid prerequisites or negative point budgets; old saves receive the correct points; every gear change has a visible model result.

    Expedition playtest + next zone

    Track actual encounter time, incoming hits, evasions, resources spent, promotion bottlenecks, and controller errors. Then add the ridge relay and C4–6 missions.

    Acceptance: new players can explain why promotion is blocked; pulls feel deliberate; retreat remains useful; each future circle introduces a new encounter or world function.

    Multiplayer follows the single-player slice: keep combat, spawn rolls, inventory, progression, and reward receipts behind clear game functions now. Later, a server owns those decisions; clients send input and display outcomes. This page does not claim the prototype has networking.
